The problems I mostly face working at a semiconductor company are very domain-specific. There's not much information out in the world, and little to refer to, so you have to extrapolate from existing material. That makes them really hard, but solving them is very rewarding. It's the same satisfaction you get from solving a four-point question on Korea's college entrance exam. A pretty instinctive sense of accomplishment.

So I thought about whether I could really get skin in the game[^1] by diving deep into decision-making. The company's organizational structure is still pretty legacy, so even if I'm lucky and become a team lead, the best I could do is make small decision fragments. To be a real decision-making player, you'd have to climb much higher. And since the problems are "given," I don't feel like tackling them readily. If I actually start, I'll work hard, but getting going always feels forced.

In short, the problems I meet at work aren't aligned with me at all. ~~(That’s separate from how fun they are when you're working hard and solving them.)~~ They're enjoyable while I'm solving them, but afterwards I sometimes wonder what it all means. Still, I don't completely lack the desire to solve them. It's ambivalent and complicated.

I feel this way because the problems the company gives me carry, indirectly, things I want to solve. I want to be a builder. I never thought I could be a builder in my life. Yet now I'm doing the sales/marketing role I once vaguely imagined while going through engineering work like an empty shell. And while doing the sales/marketing work I imagined in the past, when I'm vibe-coding, I vaguely imagine I might become an AI builder. ~~(Hooray for the AI world)~~

Believing that, as yesterday's choices became today, today's designs will become tomorrow.

It's so hard to find what I really want. I'm wrapped up in self-referential knots I don't even notice, buried and covered, so I have to dig it out with everything I've got. Every question circles back to me. "Find the problem I want to solve and solve it." That's why I read books, write, share with people, connect with smart folks, and build something.

Let's do it my way. Do it your way.
