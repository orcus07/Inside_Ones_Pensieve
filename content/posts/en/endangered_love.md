### The Secretary Problem and Optimal Stopping Theory
I stumbled upon something called the Secretary Problem. It's a thought experiment where you interview several candidates one by one to hire a secretary, but you can't go back and choose a candidate you've already passed on.

According to mathematical proof, the optimal strategy is to interview 37% of the total candidates, and then hire the first person who's better than anyone you've seen so far. So, to find the best secretary, I only need to meet 37% of them, right?

![image](/images/endangered_love_img1.png)

Unfortunately, this strategy only gives you about a 35% chance of hiring the absolute best secretary. What about hiring a secretary in the top 10%? The success rate jumps to 80%. ~~(And the number of necessary attempts even drops to about 14%.)~~ What we can learn here is that a strategy to get the second-best might actually be the best, rather than an optimal strategy to get the absolute best.

But I wasn't very satisfied with that conclusion. Why not?

It's because I was mostly the me I already knew. It was extremely rare for me to be surprisingly excellent in an unexpected way. Rediscovering myself as handsome, cool, and lovable usually happened through others. So, if possible, I'd want the person looking at me to see me as the best. Rather than choosing me because they're used to a situation where only second-bests are left, thinking, "This is good enough." 2 Crucially, love isn't about stopping at a mathematically optimal moment; it's a realm of human emotion and intuition.

Long ago, love, like Prometheus's fire, brought warm happiness to everyone just by its existence. But now, it seems this kind of love is truly endangered.

Now, we only share a certain amount of time with each other. We're temporarily connected by some occasion, in some place, with some thing, but in reality, we don't know each other well. The space where we can open our hearts and talk, where sincerity and love can spread, seems to be narrowing to a safe range determined by something or someone we don't even know. When someone bravely expresses their true feelings, I hope the people around them don't find it so unfamiliar, awkward, or embarrassing to call it sincerity, but rather a shell. I hope it's a comfortable environment where even laughter is careful, lest it wound that small act of courage.

Back in the day. They say each person had one love. An endangered love that humanity will lose tomorrow. ~~(Lee Chan-hyuk is truly a genius. Let Chan-hyuk do whatever he wants)~~
