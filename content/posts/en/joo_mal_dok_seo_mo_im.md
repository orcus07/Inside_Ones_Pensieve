We decided to start a small weekend reading club within the company. I set up recruitment criteria as below and sent out a CTA invitation email. The acceptance rate was 42.8%, which I'm incredibly satisfied with. I'm documenting the recruitment criteria and the full recruitment message here. I even came up with a working title, "Sticky Reading Club," hoping for a loose yet strong connection, like sticky notes that you attach and detach.

> Recruitment Criteria
> 
> 1. Those who are sincere in their work and pursue truth.
> 2. Those who have planned and driven their own projects.
> 3. Those who have achieved small innovations through immersion in daily life.


Hello, TL.

How have you been doing lately?

I often knock on the doors of other professions out of curiosity. What I've realized every time I go out these days is that people are much more curious about my profession than I am about theirs. It's surprisingly new to me that so many people find my work more interesting than I do. My partner, who's a designer, even asks about the difference between DDR5 and HBM.

And I often recall the conversations I used to have with you, TL, while working together. Sometimes, those precious conversations just evaporate as soon as they're spoken. It's a mistake I make every time, but when I'm with everyone, I always feel like that time will last forever. So, I've been thinking a lot about how you're doing, what you're spending most of your time on, and if there's anything I can help with, which I didn't get to ask.

I've rambled on a bit. What I'm trying to say is, I'm thinking of starting a reading club. 📚

I believe that as much as we diligently and busily go to work, we also need to ask ourselves various questions and answer them. For example, things like these:

1. How do I work, and what senses are needed in my profession?
2. Have I ever achieved small innovations through immersion in daily life?
3. What is Jeff Bezos's Customer Obsession?
4. What is Elon Musk's First Principle Thinking?

These topics are really cringey, but sometimes I think they're important. No, I think they *must* be important from now on.

But honestly, even I don't talk about them enough at work. There's no one to talk to, and I'm so caught up in daily life and my main job that I don't have enough opportunities to think for myself. There have been many times when I thought I understood something, but in reality, I had just memorized what someone else had written or said, without truly thinking it through.

So, I bravely wrote this letter again to the TLs whose farewells I regretted. We take our colleagues for granted, just like we go to work every day, but in fact, our allotted time is limited. That's why I wanted to have these conversations more comfortably and deeply outside of work.
