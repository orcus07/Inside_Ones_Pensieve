If you're interested in the world of AI, you've probably heard about OpenAI's recent circular investment structure (see image below). According to the Financial Times, OpenAI has already secured long-term contracts worth approximately $1.4 trillion. _(Just for reference, to give you a sense of scale, South Korea's total GDP in 2017 was $1.6 trillion.)_ Given that most partners and banking consortiums are rushing into this unprecedented funding, many people seem to be worried it's FOMO or a bubble, reminiscent of how folks used to shout "Gajua!" during the crypto boom.

![e9004ac1-c663-4798-bc3a-1a8cbd027789_700x483.png](/images/korgnas_korgnas_ai_news_clipping_25_11_28_img1.png)

Historically, a similar case to today's situation was the telecom/data center bubble in the early 2000s. However, unlike then, when demand was virtually non-existent, OpenAI, along with Anthropic and Gemini, are showing significant traffic conversion in the B2B market (see image below). This makes you think that AI isn't just some wild fantasy. _(And once you try it yourself, it's really hard to go back.)_

![e2ce2152-15ca-4b7f-9724-fc9486cdba2f_475x634.jpeg](/images/korgnas_korgnas_ai_news_clipping_25_11_28_img2.jpg)

Try to get rid of the elephant in your head after hearing the phrase, "Don't think of an elephant now." Strangely, it doesn't disappear; instead, you think about it even more, and it's hard to erase. Human thoughts are quite influenced by frameworks and external factors. When you hear "bubble," an uneasy feeling that it might burst follows. But if you hear "supply and demand mismatch," productive solutions like how to adjust and change the supply chain follow. A pragmatic attitude is more constructive than black-and-white logic.
