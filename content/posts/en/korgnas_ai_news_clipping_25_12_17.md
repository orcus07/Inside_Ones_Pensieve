# An Unscientific Person's AI News Clipping 25/12/17

### DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis: Solving the Root Node Problem

The Root Node Problem is a term widely used in computer science. It's a problem where the starting point of a tree structure (see figure below) significantly impacts the overall outcome. For your information, tree structures are embedded in many aspects of our daily lives. For example, Windows folders and web pages are all built with tree structures.

![](/images/korgnas_korgnas_ai_news_clipping_25_12_17_img1.jpg)

This Root Node Problem has recently gained attention due to AI algorithms. The generative AI launched by ChatGPT is now looking towards the Agentic / AGI / ASI era. Wealthy companies are investing astronomical sums to curb hallucination and enhance models to be larger and more efficient. As a result, AI now demonstrates overwhelming capabilities in many areas, surpassing humans. After defeating Lee Sedol in 2016, AI now wins gold medals in the International Mathematical Olympiad and can replace most coding personnel.

![](/images/korgnas_korgnas_ai_news_clipping_25_12_17_img2.jpg)

However, a major problem AI still faces is a lack of consistency. It's called Jagged Intelligence. While it excels at a doctoral level in specific domains, it makes trivial mistakes in basic logic in other areas. Humans can live much more generally and logically in the real world, but AI cannot live alone.

Currently, this is compensated by extending the inference time (Scaling Time), but Demis Hassabis points out that even this improvement doesn't re-verify inference results or content, and it doesn't effectively utilize thinking time. He mentions that it's only about 50% improved. Another problem is that once an AI is trained and deployed, it can undergo some fine-tuning or information updates, but it doesn't continuously learn by living in the world like humans do.

The true leap to AGI or ASI might come when AI begins to understand physical contexts—not just language, video, or images—and can continuously reinforce experiential learning through feedback loops.
