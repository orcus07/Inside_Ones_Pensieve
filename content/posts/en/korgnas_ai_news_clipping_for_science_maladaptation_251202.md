### Apple: Hey Siri! Bye Siri

![cover image](/images/korgnas_ai_news_clipping_for_science_maladaptation_251202_img1.jpg)

John Giannandrea, Apple's head of AI, is retiring in the spring of '26. The launch of Siri's AI version has been repeatedly delayed, and there have been issues with team management and investment priorities.

You might remember that Siri was actually the last project Steve Jobs kicked off. It was the most innovative and impressive feature at the iPhone 4S keynote in 2011. Why hasn't Apple, which promised to provide a seamless customer experience as a voice assistant, improved Siri in 15 years?

Ben Thompson points out that the Apple Intelligence Apple wanted to create had its differentiation in their strengths: security and private data protection. He suggests that trying to showcase all of this at once with Siri was a factor in its failure. The DNA of Apple, which created the disruptive innovation of the smartphone, ironically acted lukewarmly towards launching a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) that the market might have wanted. _(Does it really take 15 years?_)

Looking back at my experience as an Apple fanboy since the iPhone 3, I've often been genuinely impressed by the subtle details of Apple products. For them, who perfectly design, execute, and brand everything from marketing to product launch, perhaps releasing an unsatisfactory product that only offers basic features to customers was unacceptable.

Just as it's hard for people to live their lives clashing with the world according to their own convictions, it seems truly difficult for great companies to innovate while maintaining their philosophy in a changing paradigm.
