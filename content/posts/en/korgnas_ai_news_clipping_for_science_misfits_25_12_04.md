Memory manufacturer Micron is reportedly phasing out its “Crucial” consumer brand and expanding its production capacity for AI and cloud infrastructure. In other words, memory is now too valuable to sell to consumers.

The PC market was once a symbol of DIY innovation, but its growth stopped long ago. Leading companies barely maintain their businesses with profit margins of around 5%. Until now, the business managed to survive by relying on an efficient Taiwanese supply chain. However, in the massive wave of AI, the old PC supply chain is disappearing and being reorganized.

Everyday consumers can, and should, have the ability to improve their computers through do-it-yourself memory upgrades. - Crucial -

Crucial’s vision and mission—the freedom to improve and upgrade one’s own PC—don’t resonate anymore. It seems consumers have become quite lazy. I guess I should buy an iPhone or MacBook before their prices go up next year. Or should I wait for Jony Ive to release a sleek, AI-native device after OpenAI’s Project Red is complete?
