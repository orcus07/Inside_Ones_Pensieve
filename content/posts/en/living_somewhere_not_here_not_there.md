I recently realized I speak my dialect from my belly, and Seoul speech comes out of my throat. It's probably a leftover from spending my school years in Gwangju (where I grew up) and then living alone in Seoul — a city that takes no prisoners. 
I've lived in a place that's neither city nor countryside, somewhere I can't really pin down. I always hovered around the mainstream — never quite part of it, and never proudly walking as an outsider either. I tried to look comfortable beside the mainstream, while all the while I was busy trying to fly straight toward somewhere else. Now I live like a Seoul person so seamlessly that neither I nor anyone else notices. But the distance between who I was and who I am now is as wide as the space between my belly and my throat.
These days I try to bring my head closer to my heart. It's a kind of alignment — matching my body in the present with my thoughts. I haven't gone fishing, but when my head, body, and heart connect in a flash, it feels like I've landed a trophy catch. I line myself up like that, and then I give and share with whoever's around — no strings attached.
Eventually, that reaches someone's heart. Unconditional goodwill and reciprocation flow between us, and a strong trust grows. That's how I become you, and we become us.
