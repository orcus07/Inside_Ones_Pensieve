I joined the company as an engineer at a time when the height of a horse was important.The job was to find product defects.At the time, the top-down culture had very clear boundaries between roles and responsibilities.Seniors take over the work, and juniors learn and train as apprentices.In the semiconductor business, governed by Moore's Law and supply and demand curves, most problems were predictable.New problems could also be solved using old rules of experience.All answers and solutions were found in the experiences of our seniors.

At this time, there was no need to think anew, and learning from experience was the key to my development.If you nod from above, the person below will also nod, and if you shake your head, it is polite to bow together.Relationships naturally formed from top to bottom, like gravity.Rather, there is something comfortable about it.Knowledge and achievements accumulate over time, and it feels like we are heading to the same destination on a big bus together, giving us a strange sense of stability.

But I found it very uncomfortable.Everyone said it was comfortable, but it was really unusual.

So I changed my job to B2B marketing.Our company's marketing prepares for the future by analyzing current/potential markets and identifying demand consistency.Dealing with uncertainty and engaging in decision-making is the essence of work.Unlike defect analysis where there was a correct answer, most phenomena were unpredictable, old rules of thumb were often wrong, and decisions were always overturned.The knowledge I learned as an engineer was completely useless, and I had to learn everything anew.

Meanwhile, people's appearances were very diverse.Give up and rely on someone (people who have lost their wild nature), intentionally not try your best (because it will change anyway), minimize the intensity and total amount of work (work hard only at the end), repeat indefinitely until someone convinces you, etc.There were few seniors or mentors who shined like the North Star, and the tasks that needed to be done piled up in front of me.I couldn’t change my inertia or my life just by mustering up the courage to make a change “once.”

I think that was my Kairos moment.(Kairos is the son of Zeus. He is the god of opportunity. He has long hair in the front but bald on the back. It is said that if you do not seize the opportunity quickly, you will not be able to recognize it later.)

I made my own decisions and choices, when I was stuck in a dead end, I had to deal with the results myself, and when no one could or could help me, I tried to do the work around me as if it were my own.If no one teaches me, I have to learn. If no one tells me, I have to teach.I jumped into it myself without any hope or dependence.

As time passed, I became more comfortable.I gained confidence that I could do it on my own.At that time, amazingly, great people and great opportunities appeared around me.I even had the experience of collaborating with such people and leading them in a small way.Seeing the perspective I had built up being shattered by really cool people made me even more humble and had fun.I feel like a wonderful life awaits me.

Now I try to match the flow and texture of the words.They communicate through multiple personas and a mix of nods and nods and text messages.Eliminate gaps and awkwardness with an attitude as if you saw it yesterday.It is good to build trust in advance because there are no communication costs.And by transparently sharing all context and background, friction is reduced.Even if you don't trust me and don't share, open everything first.If you let us know, it will come back bigger and you can learn more.You can become excellent.

Words are about flow and texture, not pitch, and I may be able to do everything alone, but together we excel.It's a grateful life.
