My neighborhood is basically a mountain. Walking paths wrap around it like a soft blanket, and little outdoor gyms pop up here and there — it’s a nice place to live. I’m on a high floor, so I can enjoy the view from home.

Then, out of nowhere, I get this urge to meet a wild boar in person. I imagine it with thick, coarse bristles, sharp enough to poke through my pants, and a warm-toned gray would be perfect. For some reason, I feel like if I met this creature, I could just leap on it like that grandfather who rode a boar on Animal Farm — snap, hop on. Even if I only rode it sometimes, it would feel as comfortable as a Ddareungi (Seoul’s public bike), and we’d thump-thump straight to the station.

This is my happy hour — little daydreams like that. Afterward, my head feels clearer. I let my eyes relax and stare at a Magic Eye, and when I come back, I realize I’d been squinting the whole time. After a daydream, the tight reins around my thoughts loosen. My mind sometimes sprints off into the mountains — thump-thump — and sometimes a reality check hits or it’s just plain ridiculous, but as long as it’s a kind of mental stretch and it’s fun, that’s enough.

I even imagine a “World’s Best Daydreaming Contest” — like those zoning-out contests by the Han River. It might not become the next mountain-trout festival, but it could still be a decent hit. If every day feels like an “un-happy hour,” why not flip the switch for a bit and daydream your own happy hour?
