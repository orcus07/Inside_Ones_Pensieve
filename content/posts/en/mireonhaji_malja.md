title: "No Lingering Attachments"
date: 2026-05-05
summary: "Don't leave regrets behind."

---

I briefly jot down words, sentences, and texts that catch my eye in my iPhone's notes. I love the feeling of 'capturing' a fleeting moment. Yesterday's moments accumulate, layer by layer. But old notes often drift far from their initial sparkle and freshness. It's clear they're still good when I look at them again, and I completely understand why I captured those words and texts back then.

Even if I bravely pick up a pencil again, it's hard to readily write with old notes. It seems that keeping them for too long has caused them to be somewhat digested in my mind and thoughts. It's like a soda that's lost its fizz because I took a sip and didn't close the lid properly. Those notes, even with plenty of shelf life left, have faded in feeling. No matter how many times I look at them, they've become topics I can no longer use.

So, I should just delete them and start anew, but I keep having lingering attachments. I might have digested them well, but I'm afraid that if I delete what I've noted down, it'll be gone forever, and I won't be able to recall those thoughts again.

To start fresh and well, you need to clear things out. If you selectively forget and organize well, you won't be swayed by past or buried thoughts, and you can unfold your ideas cleanly and lightly.

I'll try not to foolishly cling to the traces of the past. Instead, I'll focus on the present and let things pass well. If I don't write it down right away, it's not truly mine anyway. Outside today, summer is already vibrant and green.
