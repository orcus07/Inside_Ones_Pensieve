---
title: "The Tree and the Shoulder"
date: 2026-01-03
summary: "Evergreen seeks good shoulders."
tags: [Essay]
---

Sometimes when I'm reserving a table at a restaurant or talking to a customer service representative, they ask for my name. But my name isn't easy for someone hearing it for the first time to catch. If you count how many times it's been mispronounced, I'd have to change my name to "Sang-nok" or "Sal-lok." ( ~~It's probably because my name contains the liquid consonant 'ㄹ'[^1], which is considered one of the hardest Korean consonants to pronounce, and it ends with a closed sound.~~ )

In those situations, I often say, "It's Sangrok, as in 'evergreen tree' (sang-rok-su)." When I make that little joke, the people on the other end of the phone finally pronounce my name correctly. I guess to all the strangers I'll meet in the future, I'll always be an evergreen tree.

It's harder than we think to understand ourselves and each other, so we often borrow things to explain ourselves to the world. But if we get too immersed in that, we end up living our lives comparing ourselves to something that's merely an example for explanation. We live by comparing our choices and judgments to imaginary beings or goals. In those moments, I'm clearly here, yet not truly here. Even if I were to reach that imaginary place, it would just become "here" again.

That's why I like Isaac Newton's expression, 'standing on the shoulders of giants'[^2]. It feels like he's saying that for me to expand, I need to stand firm on what I've discovered. It gives me a sense of agency.

However, the shoulders I like and want to climb onto aren't necessarily those of the most brilliant masters in the world, or perfectly sculpted deltoids, or what everyone calls cool and trendy. Instead, they're the accumulation of things around me. It's not just a fleeting curiosity, like in Zinov's song. When I find small and large, old accumulations nearby, I often want to climb them. I just want to run up and see.

Sangrok, as in 'evergreen tree,' is actually Sangrok, who is desperate to climb onto good shoulders.

---
[^1]: Among Korean consonants, the liquid consonant 'ㄹ' is the only one of its kind and is known to be the most difficult to pronounce, often being the last sound children and foreigners master.
[^2]: ["If I have seen further than others, it is by standing upon the shoulders of giants." — Isaac Newton in a 1675 letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_on_the_shoulders_of_giants)
