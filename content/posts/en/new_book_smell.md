When I stroll through a bookstore, nothing is more irresistible than the smell of the books. New books smell so good it's hard to walk past them. It isn't just "paper smell" — it's a layered thing. Different layers, different notes. Even different sections have different scents. The business/economics shelf, with its thick pages and hardcovers, has distinctive scents by publisher and author. The self-help/essay shelf, by contrast, tends to be less flashy in cover and cleaner, subtler in scent. Each has its own charm. I like magazines and comics too, but they're usually shrink-wrapped. I can't smell them well, so I rarely buy them.
After I pick a book that smells nice, I almost always grab one from the second or third row in from the display. I tell myself it's because nobody's touched those. I like to think I'm picking the least-handled copy. Of course, it's possible that it's exactly like a mug's handle — everyone's been touching that spot — and it's actually the most handled one. I don't care. My buying is full of prejudice anyway. As long as it makes me feel good, that's enough.
Those specially chosen books get placed around my home or office like objects or a diffuser. They're set out and enjoyed for a while. I let them sit until the scent fades. Then they wait to catch my eye again. The tables get turned. And after some time, one day a different version of me will choose that book and actually read it. It's like Cinderella's glass slipper — a day arrives when the fit is just right. The me who picks a book today becomes the me who gets picked by it tomorrow.
Choosing books is a very impulsive, subjective act. It's nothing like reading, which calls for slow attention, flipping pages, savoring. My bookshelf at home is already crammed with books that signal things — show-off reads, paper books, books from long-ago reading groups, pretty covers, gifts. The moment I step into a bookstore, all that sense of fullness at home vanishes. My head goes blank with the thought of holding another book. I can shop at a supermarket and never stop at the free sampling counter. I buy only what I mean to buy. But in a bookstore I'm always carried away by different scents. I end up with a couple of books in my hands. Is there a more irrational, self-indulgent kind of buying?
That doesn't mean I scorn used books. I don't. If it's a book I really want or one that's hard to find, I'll pay extra for it. But I can't always love the smell of used books. It's inconsistent. There's context I don't know, so it's foreign to me. Buying used books feels purposeful — it's practical — but it's not the same lush pleasure. It's a sober kind of purchase that gives only as much satisfaction as you expect. Not much fun.
So if someone asks me why I buy books, it's for the scent. It's like roasting fresh coffee beans yourself.
