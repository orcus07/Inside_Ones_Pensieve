Before I sharpen a pencil, I really need to take my time and consider if it's worth sharpening. For example, I think about whether I want it super sharp or a bit blunt, if it should be a long pencil or a short one, or if I should sharpen a pencil I've already been using or a brand new one. Only then can I put it into the sharpener. It's quite a fuss for something that should be simple. After all that deliberation, when I put it into the pencil sharpener (I use a Muji manual one), it sharpens exactly as I imagined in just a fleeting moment. The reason I enjoy this process is that I can ponder within a defined scope, and there's a peculiar feeling of immediately manifesting my chosen thoughts into a physical object. You could say it's a trial version of a thought experiment. After admiring the carefully sharpened pencil and tidying the shavings into the trash, I feel quite refreshed.

Still, I couldn't just keep sharpening pencils forever, so in September, I made up my mind and applied for a morning writing club. We meet at 6 AM and each write our own pieces for an hour, maintaining a loose, NPC-like relationship with each other. (But the highlight of this club is the cheer we shout to each other at 6:03 AM every day, and the weekly reflections we share—they're truly wonderful.) I wasn't always an NPC; I missed a few times, but I attended most sessions, gathered many thought fragments, and even managed to write a few Substack letters, which made me proud.

[](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!f0Wv!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5271d233-2267-42ee-baa7-070d79d6195c_1179x1463.jpeg)

However, one regrettable point was that at some point, my thoughts started spiraling, and I found myself spending all my time searching for material. Thoughts like, "It would be good to read this book," or "It would be even better if this idea were blended in," kept coming. One day, an entire hour passed just choosing. I was choosing thoughts and topics, just like choosing a pencil. Still, I considered that I'd done well enough and subtly mentioned this thought during the final reflection. A member who makes a living writing heard me and said that they write for one hour, and then they write for "another" hour.

Those words resonated with me, feeling so right and obvious, and they stayed in my mind. I kept choosing pencils, thinking I could sharpen them right away, but I realized that to write like a pencil sharpener, I still needed to invest more time. I thought that expressing my thoughts clearly was still a tough job for me, which is why I always put it off until the very end. Even beyond writing, doing difficult things first is always the hardest part of life. Even when you know it, you always try to avoid it. Still, I'll try a little harder not to avoid it, and be a little bolder. That's how I want to live and look at things.
