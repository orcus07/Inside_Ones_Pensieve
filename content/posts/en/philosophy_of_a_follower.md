The turning point of my life was the *Suneung*—the grueling Korean college entrance exam. My life, which had always been about "getting ahead" for the sake of test scores, suddenly started "following behind" once I achieved that ultimate life goal. The days of meticulously planting seeds and harvesting crops turned into a quiet off-season after I reached a milestone I didn't even remember setting. For the first time, I viscerally felt a sense of lack and emptiness. 

And for quite a long time, I believed in an unchanging, absolute authenticity. I vaguely yearned for it, assuming it existed out there somewhere like a hidden treasure, wandering around and dabbling in whatever caught my eye. Chasing after and copying things that looked good around me felt like part of that discovery process, and I honestly loved it. It felt like I had reclaimed my lost harvest season. I ravenously stuffed my hungry self with anything that seemed appealing. I thought that if I filled myself up completely like that, my own true self would magically appear.

But there was no such thing. No matter how much I imitated others, I didn't change. To actually transform, I have to experience and feel things for myself, one by one, even if it's slow. In a way, we're all followers—but *how* we follow determines whether it turns into a philosophy, a catalyst for growth, or our own unique path. The things we stack up layer by layer through that process might eventually create something entirely new, or perhaps even lead the way for others. It's such a profound realization that it leaves me endlessly humbled.

Imitation and interpretation follow completely different streams of thought. While imitation just chases the end result, interpretation is about drawing out the cause and effect so you can truly understand it yourself. Luckily, after a long season of imitating, I'm finally stepping into my season of interpreting. Just as the creator Kim Spi playfully wanders, and Ryo tinkers with ideas, I am going to try interpreting the world—hoping that one day, I'll find the perfect words with the exact flavor I love.
