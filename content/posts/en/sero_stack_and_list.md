One day, I woke up and scribbled "Stacking Vertically, Arranging Horizontally" into my iPhone notes. ~~(Why?)~~
At the time, I felt like I'd discovered the ultimate answer to life, the universe, and everything. The next day, looking at the note with a clear head, it felt like seeing '42' — the answer a supercomputer gave to a similar question in 'The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy'. I still don't know why I wrote those words in my sleep, but I'll use that clear sentence as a guide to connect the thoughts and contexts that followed.

First, let's start with **'stacking vertically'**. Stacking vertically is hard. Think of the board game Jenga. No matter how safely and high you stack it, it'll collapse without a trace if the base shakes even a little. And the higher you stack, the more unstable it gets.

Conversely, **'arranging horizontally'** is easy and safe. Imagine lining up dominoes continuously. You can't stack them high, but if you just place them next to each other, you can arrange them in a line as long as you want. ~~(You're probably wondering what on earth I'm talking about, so I'll get some help from GPT...)~~

![alt](/images/sero_stack_and_list_img1.jpg)

In my dream, I think I considered deeply contemplating and focusing to produce a more beautiful and perfect outcome than before as **'vertical'**. And I thought of consistently trying things, even if they weren't perfect, as **'horizontal'**.

It's really more about choosing a psychological tendency with no right answer. The former is closer to an artist/researcher, and the latter is closer to an entrepreneur/businessperson.

However, for me, producing a perfect outcome beyond expectations seems as difficult as stacking Jenga blocks (vertically) high. The way I can stay focused and enjoy what I do is to find direction and learn through various attempts, even if they're not perfect. (Learning by Doing, or like arranging dominoes horizontally)

Looking back, I've spent a lot of time doing nothing, just trying to immerse myself in the pursuit of an imagined perfect outcome. Even if it wasn't a crucial task directly related to my goals, time would just get sucked away. 1

Recently, BZCF Jigi shared a thought experiment about discretionary time. If you subtract sleeping hours from a week, you have 112 hours, and if you subtract working hours, it's about 72 hours. ~~(If you subtract eating time too, it's roughly 60 hours...)~~ Let's assume an average of about 100 hours. Then, watching shorts for an hour means you're using 1% of your time resources. Seeing it in numbers might make us more aware and focused on what we can actively avoid or reduce.

What percentage of your week do you spend where and how?

Do you stack vertically? Or do you arrange horizontally?
