#### See-Saw

I went to the Crushhour concert. The main motif of this performance was the see-saw. For Crush, the see-saw represented his fierce yesterdays. It was a past where he sang alone in an empty playground. Like a see-saw[^1] where the scenery appears (See) and then disappears (Saw) again, he was simply enduring time.

Now, with his dream having become a reality, he looks back at the past. Crush can now reminisce about his past. He can sing about new beginnings, not just the end of the past. Even if the scenery isn't visible, he's no longer anxious; instead, he feels excited and thrilled. He no longer just endures time, but spends it gratefully and preciously with everyone around him. ~~(The dedication video to people of all ages and foreigners at the end of the concert was truly wonderful.)~~ Thanks to Crush's nostalgic reflections, we also got the bonus of hearing a surprise guest, Zion.T, feature ~~(I wonder if you know the song "See Through"... if you do, you're definitely a 20th-century person).~~

Perhaps a dream becoming reality is that moment when the conviction I've built reaches beyond me to someone else.

_**I see through U**_

* * *

#### Good Goodbye

Crushhour had Hwasa as a guest. As everyone expected, she sang her recently viral song, 'Good Goodbye.' I'm the type who needs to hum the melody first to delve deeper into a song, but perhaps because I'd watched many YouTube videos beforehand, I focused on the lyrics from the start.

Everything has an end. But having an end isn't sad. If you think about it, it's actually a good thing. A heavy commute is for a light departure, a marathon runner's high comes from finishing, and all ex-boyfriends/girlfriends are just seasonal connections meant to lead to a lifelong partner. An end is the driving force that makes us live in the present, not procrastinate, and feel fulfilled.

They say every farewell includes the word 'good.' Like "Good"-bye, or "잘"-가 (jal-ga, meaning 'go well'). As Hwasa said, even if it hurts when we reach the end, let's send it off 'gracefully.'

**Goodbyes hurt us, but they'll be graceful.**

[^1]: The origin of the Korean word '시소' (siso) is said to be a phonetic transcription of 'Seesaw.' There's a popular misconception that it was named 'siso' because when you ride it, the scenery appears (See) and then disappears (Saw). However, it's more likely that the shape of riding a see-saw resembles sawing, and the name is an English phonetic transcription of the sound of sawing.
