“When I was little, I really envied that friend. He had all kinds of friends, all sorts of interests, and the girls liked him too. That was when I started copying him.”

I got this far and it was already so boring. This sort of autobiographical anecdote is exactly what I'd tell people not to write when helping with their personal statements in a job-hunting study group. It wouldn't even make the top three examples of what not to do. Yet whenever I decide to write and try to pour out all those thoughts sloshing around in my head, a dull intro like this constricts the flow like a narrow faucet.

We've moved beyond the great age of Shorts. Now long-form pieces and longer videos do well too. I want to write with the light touch and overflowing huu-mor of writers like Lee Seul-ah and Kim Saseum, or those graceful poets. So I turn, rather desperately, to AI, only to end up back at the stiff, uncool prose that only I can write. — When I asked AI, it advised me to \*\***practice looking at ordinary things in a slightly offbeat way**\*\*. Oh, thanks. —

I'm copying so many things that the disclaimers alone could fill a page in Word. It's embarrassing, but I still can't stop. I've discovered so many lovely, cool things that I've taken to yelling at my fingertips, ruled by their uncool muscle memory, to change. I'm not asking for much. I just want it to look cool to me. Please, move. Come on, my pencil lead.
